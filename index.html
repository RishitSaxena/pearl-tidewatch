Useful-work fingerprint

matrix shapes from each block's PoUW certificate
Demand side: models sold on the Pearl Inference Platform ($ per 1M tokens)

A block's certificate records the shape of the matrix multiplication that won it. Shapes that match transformer layer sizes (for example Llama-70B's 8,192 hidden and 28,672 feed-forward widths) suggest the block came out of real model inference, Pearl's "2-for-1" design. Power-of-two shapes come from pure-mining software. Miners choose their shapes, so read this as a signal rather than proof.

Inside the pools

Kryptex, HeroMiners, LuckyPool and JetSki pool APIs
Round effort: work done since each pool's last block, as a share of what a block needs (line = 100%)

Effort resets to zero when the pool finds a block. Rounds far past 100% are bad luck, or hashrate the pool reports but doesn't have. Kryptex publishes no live round, so its row is the average over its last blocks.

How this dashboard works

method notes and caveats
Where the numbers come from and how fresh they are

A collector running on the owner's machine keeps WebSocket connections to SafeTrade, BigONE, Ourbit and CoinEx, follows every new Pearl block through the node RPC and Blockbook, and polls prlscan, PearlBridge, Uniswap (via Ethereum RPC), the OTC desks and a handful of context APIs. It writes one snapshot every few seconds; snapshots are pushed into this page's shared database, and every open copy of the page updates the moment a new one lands. The pill in the top bar says Live while snapshots are under about two minutes old.

Markets. Most exchange tickers called "PRL" belong to Perle, a different token, so markets are matched by exchange id. CoinEx lists Pearl as PEARL/USDT with deposits disabled, so it trades well below the other venues and is left out of the volume-weighted price. The CEX tape keeps up to 26 hours of trades; windows shorter than that are labelled.

Supply and hashrate. Supply comes from the consensus formula S(h) = 2.1B · h / (h + 650,226), not from aggregators, whose circulating figures lag. One Pearl hash is one int7 multiply-accumulate, so hashrate is difficulty-implied work per second.

Exchange flows. SafeTrade has no fixed hot wallet. It spends customer deposit addresses directly and sends change to a fresh address each time. Its addresses are inferred by clustering addresses that are spent together, starting from about 14,000 known ones. A new customer's first deposit is only recognised once SafeTrade spends it, so the 24-hour figures can be revised upward. BigONE and CoinEx each reuse one hot wallet.

Useful work. The share of "model-shaped" blocks compares each block's proof-of-work matrix dimensions with known transformer layer sizes. Miners choose their shapes, so treat it as a signal, not an audit.

Pools and miners. Pool shares come from coinbase tags and each pool's API. Round effort and luck are what the pools report. Kryptex's top-miner list contains two aggregates, everyone mining to an e-mail account and everyone using the Kryptex app, together about 30,000 unrelated workers; they are excluded from "largest single miner". Rented-GPU break-even uses WhatToMine's per-card hashrates and the median of the 10 cheapest verified Vast.ai offers, smoothed over an hour.

Reach and adoption. Follower, member, download and holder counts are proxies for attention and setup activity, not user counts. Pearl has no official Telegram; the groups counted are community-run.

Not advice. Third-party feeds can lag, break or be wrong. Check the Metric index for each number's source and age before acting on it.
